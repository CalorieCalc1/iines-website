How to Remove Leaked Content From the Internet

Published 5 July 2026 · Updated 5 August 2026 · 8 min read

When private photos, videos, or content leak, every hour counts. The difference between something being contained and something spreading everywhere is how fast and how correctly you act in the first 48 hours. This guide presents the steps that work to remove leaked content from the internet, how fast each step takes, what it costs, and the mistakes that make the situation worse.

What should you do before removing leaked content?

Before anything is removed, you need evidence. Screenshot every location the content appears, save the URLs, and record the dates. You will need this to prove ownership, file takedowns, and escalate if a site refuses to cooperate. Removing something without documenting it first can make later removals harder. IINES documents every instance of leaked content as the first step in its emergency removal service, creating an evidence trail that supports both takedown notices and any legal proceedings.

How do you establish copyright ownership of leaked content?

Copyright ownership is the single most powerful lever you have for removing leaked content. In most leak situations, whoever created the content owns its copyright — the person who took the photo or recorded the video. That ownership is what lets you force removal under copyright law, no matter which country the website is in.

If you or your client created the material, you almost certainly hold the copyright, which means a DMCA takedown is available to you. IINES verifies copyright ownership as part of every leaked content case, because this determines which legal tools are available for removal.

How do you remove leaked content from the source and search engines simultaneously?

A common mistake is to focus only on the website hosting the content. Even if that site is slow or refuses, you can cut off most of the damage by removing it from search results. Which platform-specific reporting channels work fastest?

Beyond copyright, most major platforms have dedicated processes for non-consensual and private content that are often faster than a legal notice. The table below lists the main platforms and their specific reporting channels.
