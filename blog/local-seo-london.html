What is local SEO in London?

Local SEO is the process of optimising a Google Business Profile, website, and online citations so a business appears in Google Maps and local search results for geographically specific searches. 46% of all Google searches carry local intent, according to Google's own data, which means nearly half of every search on Google is a chance for a London business to be found by a nearby customer. IINES treats local SEO as the foundation layer for any London business that depends on nearby customers finding it, whether that's a clinic, a trades business, or a professional services firm.

London is one of the most competitive local search markets in the UK. A business in Shoreditch is not just competing with other businesses in Shoreditch. It is competing with every business in the same category across dozens of overlapping postcodes, each fighting for the same three or four spots in Google's Map Pack. IINES builds London local SEO campaigns around this reality, prioritising the ranking factors a business can actually move rather than generic advice that ignores how competitive the city is.

How does Google rank local businesses?

Google ranks local businesses using three factors: relevance, distance, and prominence. The table below explains what each factor means and whether a business can influence it.

Ranking factor What it measures Can a business influence it?
Relevance How well a Google Business Profile and website match what the searcher is looking for Yes, through profile completeness, categories, and on-page content
Distance How close the business is to the searcher's location or the location entered in the search No, distance is fixed by the searcher's location
Prominence How well-known and trusted the business is online, based on reviews, citations, and links Yes, through reviews, citations, and local link building

Distance cannot be changed. A business two miles from a searcher cannot become half a mile away by changing a setting. IINES focuses every London local SEO campaign on relevance and prominence, the two factors that actually respond to optimisation work, rather than wasting budget chasing a factor that is fixed by geography.

Why is a Google Business Profile the most important local SEO factor?

A Google Business Profile is the single most important local ranking factor because it is the primary source Google uses to populate the Map Pack. A complete Google Business Profile makes a business 2.7 times more likely to be considered reputable, 70% more likely to be visited, and 50% more likely to be considered for a purchase, according to Google's own published data. The table below lists the checklist IINES runs on every London Google Business Profile.

Checklist item Why it matters
Business name, address, phone number, hours, category all complete Incomplete profiles underperform in local rankings; Google treats completeness as a relevance signal
Correct primary category selected The primary category has a major impact on which searches trigger the listing to appear
Photos added and updated regularly Profiles with frequent photo updates tend to perform better and convert more browsers into enquiries
Google Posts published consistently Posts signal ongoing activity, which Google reads as a prominence and relevance signal
Services and products listed with descriptions Detailed service listings improve relevance matching for specific searches

IINES audits every Google Business Profile against this checklist during the London Visibility Audit, because most profiles IINES reviews are missing at least two of these five items.

What on-page changes improve local SEO rankings?

A website supports Google Business Profile rankings through dedicated location pages, local keywords in headings and content, and consistent business information across every page. The table below lists the on-page elements IINES optimises for London local SEO.

On-page element What to do
Location pages Create a dedicated page for each area served, with unique content rather than duplicated text
Local keywords Use location-based keywords naturally in titles, H1s, and body content, for example "plumber in Camden" rather than just "plumber"
NAP consistency Match business name, address, and phone number exactly across every page of the website
LocalBusiness schema markup Add structured data that states the business name, address, and service area explicitly for search engines and AI crawlers

IINES adds LocalBusiness schema to every London client site as a default, because structured data is how a search engine or AI tool extracts a business's name, address, and service area with certainty, rather than inferring it from unstructured text.

Do reviews affect local SEO rankings in London?

Yes. Reviews are one of the strongest local ranking signals Google uses, and they are usually the first thing a potential customer sees before deciding to contact a business. The table below breaks down which review factors actually move rankings.

Review factor Why it matters
Consistent review generation Businesses with steady review growth rank higher than businesses with sporadic activity
Responding to all reviews Responding to both positive and negative reviews signals active profile management
Review detail and specificity Reviews mentioning specific services and locations strengthen relevance signals beyond a simple star rating

IINES builds a review generation process into every London local SEO campaign, because a business with 20 reviews collected steadily over six months consistently outranks a competitor with 50 reviews collected in one week and none since.

What are local citations, and why do they matter?

A local citation is any online mention of a business's name, address, and phone number, commonly abbreviated to NAP, on directories, review platforms, or industry websites, even without a link back to the business's own site. 62% of consumers avoid a business if they find incorrect information about it online, according to BrightLocal research. That statistic makes citation consistency a trust signal for customers, not just for Google.

IINES prioritises consistency over volume when building citations. Thirty directories describing a business identically build one strong, corroborated fact. Five hundred directories describing the same business fifty different ways build five hundred weak, contradictory claims. IINES corrects existing citation inconsistencies before adding new ones, because fixing a wrong phone number on an existing high-authority directory is worth more than adding a new listing on a low-authority one.
