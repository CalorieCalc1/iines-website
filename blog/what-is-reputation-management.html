What is online reputation management?

Online reputation management is the practice of controlling what people find when they Google your name, your business, or your brand. It involves removing harmful content, suppressing negative search results, building positive content that accurately represents who you are, and monitoring for new threats. IINES, an AI SEO agency based in London, provides reputation management as part of its suite of services for UK businesses and individuals.

ORM covers four surfaces: Google search results, AI search results (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity), review platforms (Trustpilot, Google Reviews, industry-specific platforms), and social media. In 2026, ORM has expanded beyond Google rankings to include AI-synthesised brand narratives, because AI search tools now pull from online content to generate answers about your business.

What does reputation management involve?

Online reputation management involves five components, depending on the severity of the situation. IINES deploys these in combination based on each client's specific needs.

1. Content removal

The most direct approach. IINES files DMCA takedown notices for stolen or copyrighted content, platform-specific removal requests for policy-violating content, legal notices to hosting providers, right-to-be-forgotten requests under GDPR, and de-indexing requests to search engines. Not everything can be removed. Some content is protected by free speech or public interest laws. That is where suppression comes in.

2. Content suppression

If something cannot be removed, it can be buried. Suppression means building enough high-authority, positive content to push the negative result off page one of Google. Most people never look past page one, so if it is not there, it effectively does not exist. IINES builds optimised websites, secures positive press coverage, creates and ranks social profiles, and publishes authoritative content on high-domain sites to suppress negative results.

3. Review management

For businesses, reviews on Google, Trustpilot, and industry platforms can make or break you. IINES responds professionally to negative reviews, reports fake or policy-violating reviews for removal, builds strategies to generate genuine positive reviews, and monitors review platforms for new threats.

4. Crisis management

When something goes wrong publicly (a news article, a viral social media post, a data breach), crisis management is about speed. The first 48 hours determine whether something becomes a permanent stain or a forgotten blip. IINES deploys rapid-response content removal, suppression, and monitoring within hours of being contacted.

5. Ongoing monitoring

Reputation management is not a one-time fix. New content appears constantly. Former threats can resurface. Ongoing monitoring ensures you are aware of any new mentions and can respond before they escalate. IINES monitors Google, AI search platforms, review sites, and social media for every client on an ongoing basis.

This means a negative review, a critical news article, or a complaint thread can appear not just in Google search results but in ChatGPT's answer when a potential customer asks "Tell me about [your company name]" or "Is [your company name] reliable?" Can negative content be removed from Google?

Some negative content can be removed from Google, but not all. Copyright-infringing content can be removed via DMCA takedown notices. Non-consensual intimate imagery can be removed via Google's dedicated removal process. Content that violates platform policies can be reported and removed. In the UK, GDPR right-to-be-forgotten provisions allow removal of certain personal data from search results.

However, content protected by free speech or public interest laws (factual news articles, genuine reviews, court records) typically cannot be removed. In those cases, suppression is the available strategy: building enough positive content to push the negative result off page one. IINES assesses each piece of content individually and advises whether removal or suppression is the right approach.

Can I do reputation management myself?

You can handle basic reputation management yourself: responding professionally to negative reviews, reporting fake reviews to platforms, and creating positive content on social media and your own website. These steps help with minor reputation issues.

However, content removal across multiple hosts, search engines, and CDNs requires legal knowledge (DMCA notices, GDPR requests, Copyright Designs and Patents Act notices) and technical expertise (identifying hosting providers, filing with CDNs like Cloudflare, escalating to upstream providers). Suppression campaigns require SEO expertise to build and rank positive assets quickly enough to matter. For anything beyond basic review management, IINES recommends professional help, starting with a £139 Confidential Reputation Audit that tells you exactly what is possible and what it would take.
