Zero-Click Search in 2026: Why 68% of Google Searches Send No Traffic

Published 24 June 2026 · Updated 5 August 2026 · 8 min read

68% of Google searches end without a click in 2026, up from 58.5% in 2024. When a Google AI Overview appears, that number rises to 83%. For B2B companies that rely on organic search traffic for leads, this is a fundamental shift in how business gets won online. This guide explains why zero-click search disproportionately affects B2B companies, what it means for your metrics, and how to adapt before your competitors do.

What is zero-click search?

A zero-click search is when someone Googles something and gets their answer directly on the results page without clicking through to any website. Google provides the answer through AI Overviews (AI-generated summaries at the top of the page), featured snippets (boxed answers), knowledge panels (sidebar info for companies and people), People Also Ask boxes, and direct answer cards.

In 2026, 68% of Google searches end this way, according to SparkToro and Similarweb data. The user gets their answer and leaves. Your website, even if it ranks number 1, never gets visited. Why does zero-click search hit B2B harder than B2C?

Zero-click search disproportionately affects B2B companies for four reasons. The table below compares B2B and B2C exposure to zero-click search.

Factor B2C B2B
Search volume per keyword High (10,000+ for "best running shoes") Low (500 for "best CRM for mid-market")
Impact of losing 68% of clicks Still 3,200 visitors from 10,000 searches Only 160 visitors from 500 searches
Query types Transactional ("buy running shoes") Informational and comparison ("HubSpot vs Salesforce")
AI Overview frequency Lower for transactional queries Higher for informational and comparison queries
Buying cycle Shorter, often single decision-maker 70% of research done before contacting vendor, multiple stakeholders

B2B buyers do 70% of their research before ever contacting a vendor. They Google questions like "What does an SEO retainer cost?" or "What is the difference between HubSpot and Salesforce?" If Google answers those questions directly through an AI Overview, the buyer never lands on your site to see your pitch, your case studies, or your contact form. How do AI Overviews affect B2B search traffic?

Google's AI Overviews appear most frequently for informational and comparison queries, which are exactly the type of searches B2B buyers make. When an AI Overview appears on a results page, organic click-through rates drop by 34 to 61%.

This means a B2B comparison article that used to drive 500 visits per month may now drive 150 to 250 visits, because the AI Overview answers the question above your listing. Your content is still ranking, but Google is answering the question before the user ever scrolls to your result. How to adapt to zero-click search as a B2B company

The goal shifts from "get clicks" to "get mentioned." IINES implements five strategies for B2B clients to adapt to zero-click search.

1. Optimise to be the answer, not just rank near it

Structure your content to directly answer questions in a format Google can extract. Use query-string headings, 40 to 60 word answer summaries, and comparison tables. Implement schema markup (FAQPage, BlogPosting, Organization) so Google can parse your content. IINES writes all client content using this structure, designed for AI Overview extractability.

2. Build brand recognition, not just traffic

If someone sees your brand mentioned in a Google AI Overview or a ChatGPT response, they may not click today. But when they are ready to buy, they will remember the name. Zero-click is not zero-impression. IINES builds brand entity recognition so that your company name appears in AI-generated answers even when no click occurs.

3. Own the entities in your space

When Google generates an AI Overview about "best B2B marketing agencies in London," it pulls from entities it recognises. If your brand is a recognised entity with clear associations (service, location, reputation), you get mentioned. IINES builds entity profiles through structured data, consistent brand information, and authoritative mentions.

4. Diversify into AI search

AI search engines (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity) are a growing secondary channel with different ranking factors than Google. AI SEO is a stream of inbound traffic most B2B companies have not tapped into yet. IINES builds visibility across all major AI search platforms for its clients.

5. Ensure your content is readable by AI crawlers

AI crawlers like GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot do not execute JavaScript. If your website relies on JavaScript to render key content, AI systems never see it. IINES builds websites using static architecture, where all content is present in the initial HTML, making it inherently readable by both Google's AI Overviews and standalone AI search tools.
