Technical SEO Audit Service: What's Included and Why It Matters

Published 11 August 2026 · 9 min read

A technical SEO audit is a systematic review of a website's crawlability, indexation, speed, structured data, mobile usability, and security, the infrastructure layer that determines whether Google and AI crawlers can access and understand a site at all. Content and backlinks return nothing if the pages carrying them cannot be crawled. This guide covers exactly what a technical SEO audit service checks, real 2026 UK pricing, Core Web Vitals thresholds, and the AI crawler access check most audits still skip.

What is a technical SEO audit?

A technical SEO audit is the process of reviewing a website's underlying infrastructure, everything that determines whether search engines and AI crawlers can find, access, and correctly interpret a page, separate from the quality of the content sitting on it. Across 150+ UK websites audited in 2025, 73% had Core Web Vitals failures, 61% had crawl budget issues from duplicate or thin pages, and 44% had structured data errors, and every one of those failures directly suppresses rankings regardless of how strong the content is.

IINES runs a full technical SEO audit as the foundation of every London Visibility Audit, because a business investing in content or link building before fixing broken technical foundations is spending money on work that Google may never fully credit.

What does a technical SEO audit check?

A professional technical SEO audit works through eight dimensions in a fixed order, because crawlability issues must be resolved before indexation analysis is meaningful, and indexation must be clean before structured data validation makes sense. The table below lists all eight.

Audit dimension What it checks
Crawl analysis Robots.txt rules, crawl budget waste, redirect chains, and crawl traps
Indexation review Which pages Google has indexed, and the specific cause behind any pages left out
Site speed (Core Web Vitals) LCP, INP, and CLS scores across each key page template
Mobile usability Touch target sizing, font size, viewport configuration, and intrusive interstitials
Structured data Existing schema markup validity and gaps where schema should be added
Internal linking Orphan pages, click depth, and anchor text distribution
Security (HTTPS) SSL certificate validity, mixed content, and HSTS configuration
Monitoring setup Uptime, crawl health, Core Web Vitals, and Search Console alerting

IINES covers all eight dimensions in every London Visibility Audit, then adds a ninth check most audit services still skip entirely: whether AI crawlers can access the site at all.

What are Core Web Vitals, and why do they matter?

Core Web Vitals are three confirmed Google ranking metrics measuring loading speed, interactivity, and visual stability. Pages in the top loading-speed quartile see meaningfully higher organic click-through rates than pages that fail these thresholds. The table below lists each metric and its pass criteria.

Metric What it measures Good Poor
LCP (Largest Contentful Paint) How long the largest visible element takes to load Under 2.5 seconds Over 4.0 seconds
INP (Interaction to Next Paint) How quickly the page responds to a user interaction Under 200 milliseconds Over 500 milliseconds
CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift) How much visible content shifts around during loading Under 0.1 Over 0.25

IINES measures Core Web Vitals using real user field data rather than lab simulations, because Google's own ranking signal is drawn from real Chrome user data, not a one-off speed test score.

What structured data should a technical SEO audit check?

Structured data (schema markup) tells Google explicitly what a page means, enabling rich results such as star ratings, FAQ dropdowns, and sitelinks. Only 17% of the top 10 million websites implement any form of schema markup, and rich results increase organic click-through rate by 20% to 30% on average where they appear. The table below lists the schema types a technical audit should check for.

Schema type Used on Required fields
Article / BlogPosting Blog posts and news articles headline, datePublished, dateModified, author
FAQPage Pages with visible FAQ sections Question and Answer pairs matching visible text exactly
BreadcrumbList All pages with breadcrumb navigation itemListElement array with position values
LocalBusiness Location and service-area pages address, telephone, openingHours

IINES adds Article, BreadcrumbList, and FAQPage schema to every blog post it publishes, and LocalBusiness schema to every location-relevant page, as a default rather than an upsell. How long do technical SEO fixes take to improve rankings?

Quick wins, such as fixing broken redirects or removing an accidental noindex tag, can show in rankings within days once Google re-crawls the affected pages. Core Web Vitals improvements typically take 4 to 8 weeks to influence rankings, because Google's field data rolls on a 28-day measurement window. Structural fixes, such as resolving crawl budget issues on a large site, can take 2 to 4 months to fully reflect in rankings. IINES prioritises every fix by a P0 to P3 severity system in the Visibility Audit report, so a business tackles the quick wins first and sees movement before the longer structural fixes complete.

Does a technical SEO audit check AI crawler access?

A thorough technical SEO audit in 2026 checks robots.txt for AI crawler access, specifically whether GPTBot, ClaudeBot, and PerplexityBot are blocked or allowed, alongside the traditional Googlebot checks. This is the part most audit services still miss. A site can pass every traditional technical SEO check, fast Core Web Vitals, clean indexation, valid schema, and still be completely invisible to ChatGPT, Claude, and Perplexity if its robots.txt blocks AI crawlers by default, which is common on sites migrated from certain website builders and platforms that ship a restrictive default configuration.

IINES verifies AI crawler access as a standard line item in every Visibility Audit, not a separate add-on service. Local SEO and AI search visibility are converging on the same underlying technical foundation, as covered in the full AI SEO breakdown, and a robots.txt file that silently blocks AI crawlers undoes that foundation before it can do any work.

What happens if technical SEO issues are left unfixed?

Sites without ongoing technical monitoring see an average 12% organic traffic decline per quarter from accumulating unfixed issues. Mixed content and expired SSL certificates trigger Chrome security warnings that suppress click-through rates by up to 45%. A systematic internal linking audit typically surfaces 40% to 60% of pages with zero inbound internal links, orphan pages that cannot be discovered or ranked at all. IINES treats a technical SEO audit as the first step in any engagement precisely because these failures compound silently; a business rarely notices the decline until months of accumulated traffic loss are already gone.

A monthly retainer makes more sense once the foundational issues are fixed and the business needs ongoing monitoring, because new technical issues appear continuously from CMS updates, developer changes, and algorithm shifts.
