How Much Does SEO Cost for a Small Business in the UK? (2026)

Published 11 August 2026 · 10 min read

UK small business SEO typically costs £300 to £2,500 per month, depending on provider type, industry, and competition, with average UK SEO spend sitting around £3,199 per month according to Clutch's 2026 data. This guide breaks the number down further than most: cost by provider type, cost by industry, the real London pricing premium, in-house hire versus agency retainer, audit pricing by site size, and the hidden extras most quotes leave off the invoice.

How much does SEO cost by provider type?

What a business pays depends heavily on who is doing the work. The table below breaks down typical UK rates by provider type.

Provider type Monthly cost Hourly rate Best for
Freelancer (entry-level) £300 to £500 £30 to £50 Local businesses, minimal competition
Freelancer (experienced) £500 to £2,000 £50 to £150 Small businesses wanting flexibility
Small agency £600 to £2,000 £60 to £100 SMEs needing a structured approach
Mid-market agency £1,500 to £5,000 £80 to £150 Growing businesses, competitive markets
Enterprise agency £5,000 to £25,000+ £100 to £300+ Large corporations, complex sites

48% of agencies price within the £1,500 to £5,000 monthly band, which is where most small businesses land once they move past basic local SEO. IINES's Revenue Engine retainer starts from £2,100 per month, positioned within that mid-market range.

How much does SEO cost by industry?

Industry is one of the biggest cost drivers because it determines keyword competition and content requirements. The table below shows typical UK monthly costs by sector.

Industry Monthly cost Why
Local trades (plumbers, electricians, builders) £300 to £1,500 Lower competition, geographic focus, high buying intent
Hospitality / restaurants £500 to £2,500 Moderate competition, local focus, high-intent searches
Healthcare £1,500 to £5,000 Specialist knowledge, clinical accuracy, E-E-A-T signals
E-commerce £2,500 to £10,000+ Technical complexity, thousands of product pages
Financial services £3,000 to £10,000+ Regulatory compliance, competitive keywords
Legal services £3,000 to £15,000+ Extremely competitive keywords, E-E-A-T requirements, high client value

Most IINES clients fall into the local trades, hospitality, and healthcare bands, where a Revenue Engine retainer from £2,100 per month delivers genuine strategic work without the overhead that pushes agency pricing toward the top of these ranges.

Does location affect how much SEO costs?

Yes. London-based agencies typically charge 20% to 40% more than regional equivalents for the same scope of work. A campaign costing £2,000 per month from a Manchester or Birmingham agency might cost £2,500 to £3,000 from a London agency, reflecting higher operational costs rather than necessarily better results. This gap has narrowed considerably as remote SEO delivery has become the norm, so a London postcode is no longer a reliable signal of quality. IINES operates as a lean, founder-led agency and prices in line with the mid-market range regardless of London overheads.

Is an in-house SEO hire cheaper than an agency?

For most small businesses, no. The table below compares the real annual cost of each option.

Option Annual cost What it buys
In-house SEO hire £50,000+ all-in Salary (£35k to £55k+), National Insurance, pension, tool subscriptions, training, one person's skill set
Agency retainer £18,000 to £36,000 A team covering technical SEO, content, link building, and AI search, no recruitment risk

An in-house hire tends to make sense only once a business is spending enough to justify two or more full-time SEO roles. Below that point, an agency retainer buys more combined expertise per pound spent.

What hidden costs should I budget for beyond the retainer?

The headline monthly price rarely covers everything a business ends up needing. The table below lists the most common extras.

Is SEO under £500 a month worth it?

Rarely, for anything beyond very basic local SEO. Quality SEO requires premium tools, experienced staff, and genuine content and outreach work, none of which is economically viable at rock-bottom prices. To deliver anything at £200 to £300 a month, providers typically cut corners: automated tools, unvetted outsourced work, or manipulative tactics that risk a Google penalty. Recovery from a penalty caused by bad SEO commonly costs £5,000 to £50,000 and takes 6 to 12 months, which makes the initial saving irrelevant once the damage is done.
